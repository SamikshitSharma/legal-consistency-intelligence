# 2. Battle Candidate A — Amendment & Consistency Auditor

### Core question

> “Does this amendment actually remain consistent with the agreement it modifies?”

### Why GenAI is necessary

Consider:

**Original:**

> “Either party may terminate this agreement upon thirty days' written notice.”

**Amendment:**

> “Either party may terminate immediately upon material breach.”

A traditional diff can detect different text.

It cannot inherently determine that these provisions describe **different termination pathways that may coexist rather than a simple textual replacement**.

That semantic relationship is the useful AI task.

### Strengths

* Strong GenAI justification
* Strong differentiation
* Naturally supports evidence
* Naturally supports multi-document reasoning
* Good safety boundary
* Strong testing surface
* Strong engineering architecture
* Can be implemented with a relatively narrow scope
* Directly relates to comparing documents and identifying inconsistencies, both explicitly mentioned as potential challenge directions

### Weaknesses

There are several.

#### Weakness 1 — “Amendment” is narrower than the actual capability

A judge may upload:

* two contracts
* two policy versions
* agreement + addendum

We don't want the product to unnecessarily reject useful related document pairs.

#### Weakness 2 — “Contradiction” can be ambiguous

Two clauses may appear different but actually address different circumstances.

Therefore, the model must distinguish:

**Contradiction**

from

**Modification**

from

**Additional provision**

from

**Possible interaction**

from

**No meaningful relationship**

This is important.

#### Weakness 3 — False positives are extremely damaging

If the system says:

> “Conflict detected”

and the cited clauses don't actually conflict, we've created a visible trust failure.

Claude correctly identifies citation validation as a critical hard gate. 

#### Weakness 4 — It could become a fancy diff

This is the biggest product risk.

If our demo is:

> Upload A → Upload B → “Clause 4 changed”

we lose the point.

Our demo must contain a **semantic relationship that a textual diff alone cannot adequately explain**.

### Verdict

**SURVIVES.**

But only if we build **semantic relationship reasoning**, not document diffing.

# 3. Battle Candidate B — Contract Change Impact Analyzer

Core question:

> **“What changed, and what could those changes affect?”**

This is subtly different from contradiction detection.

Example:

Original:

> Payment due within 30 days.

Revised:

> Payment due within 15 days.

The system explains:

**Change:** 30 → 15 days.

**Document evidence:** Clause X.

**Interpretation:** The stated payment window has been shortened.

**Potential downstream relevance:** Other provisions referring to payment timing may need review.

### Strengths

* Extremely understandable
* Strong demo
* Useful to ordinary users
* GenAI can reason about significance
* Less dependent on the word “contradiction”
* Works with revisions, amendments, and renewals

### Weakness

“Impact” can become dangerously close to:

> **“Tell me the legal consequences.”**

We must avoid making unsupported legal conclusions.

Also, “materiality” is inherently contextual.

Claude flagged this exact issue in its analysis: materiality needs to be expressed through checkable evidence rather than an unsupported subjective label. 

### Verdict

**SURVIVES.**

Actually, this is important:

> **I think this should be incorporated into Candidate A rather than discarded.**

Because:

**Conflict detection** is one type of meaningful relationship.

**Change-impact analysis** is another.

Both can come from the same cross-document reasoning engine.

# 10. Now let's test the GenAI necessity properly

This is the question that matters most.

Suppose we have:

### Version A

> “The customer may terminate the agreement by providing 60 days' written notice.”

### Version B

> “The customer may terminate immediately if the supplier materially breaches its obligations.”

A conventional diff can show that the sentences differ.

But our system should determine:

### Finding

**The revised document introduces an additional termination circumstance.**

### Evidence

A-14
B-7

### Relationship

**Additional provision**, not necessarily contradiction.

### Interpretation

The supplied amendment introduces immediate termination in a specified circumstance, while the original provision describes termination through advance notice.

### Uncertainty

The supplied documents do not establish whether the new provision replaces or supplements the original termination mechanism.

### Question

> “How should the immediate-termination provision interact with the existing 60-day notice provision?”

That's meaningful reasoning.

And notice what we **didn't** say:

> “The amendment gives you the legal right to terminate immediately.”

That's the safety boundary.

# 11. Another adversarial example

### Original

> “Payment shall be made within 30 days of receipt of an invoice.”

### Amendment

> “Payment shall be made within 15 days.”

A naive system:

> **CONTRADICTION DETECTED**

That may be wrong.

The better reasoning is:

> **Potential modification detected.**

Evidence:

A-8 + B-3.

Interpretation:

> The amendment appears to change the payment period from 30 days to 15 days.

Uncertainty:

> The amendment does not explicitly state whether the revised period replaces the original provision.

That is substantially better.

### This gives us a crucial output taxonomy:

**Relationship**

* Modification
* Addition
* Removal
* Apparent conflict
* Clarification
* No material relationship
* Cannot determine

That is much more defensible than:

**Red / Yellow / Green**
